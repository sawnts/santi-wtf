creativity keeps me sane.

i prioritize it as much as i can. writing, sketching, collaging, and photographing are familiar pastures but, for a good chunk of this year, i’ve hardly grazed them.

## starving

i've spent most of the year in a weird creative block — a fairly depressing one. after my tortured-artist tantrum, i decided to reframe the curse as a positive. i wrote about it, and while i still believe a lot of what i wrote — there are no creative blocks, just incubation — i was missing perspective.

creativity is unexpected, yet omnipresent.

over the last few months, i’ve picked up old hobbies that "growing up" forced me to forget about.

early this year, i went to my local bike shop and picked up a new bike. a couple of days later, i was up at 3 am binge-watching bike mechanic videos on youtube. that led me to drop a few hundred on a project bike. i’ve still got a long way to go (and a lot more hundreds to spend) before i call it finished, but the late nights working and building have been some of my favorites this year — second to riding with friends, going new places, and seeing the city with fresh eyes.

like most boys, i spent the 90s and early 2000s staring at crt screens, lan partying, and teabagging noobs. gaming was life. then, in my late teens, i stumbled onto the self-help industry and got seduced by hustle culture. long story short: grind culture’s obsession with productivity, optimization, and material success beat the love for gaming out of me.

it wasn’t until last year when my then girlfriend — who had never in her life used a right stick — picked up a nintendo switch and started pouring hours into _stardew valley_, that i remembered how much genuine joy a game can bring someone, no matter their age, gender, or experience. watching her lose her worries — wandering her farm, watering her crops, wearing a smile i’d never seen on her before — reminded me how much of growing up is forgetting about the kid, and how much of healing is remembering them.

a few weeks ago, i finally pulled the trigger on a nintendo switch 2 and got lost in the magic of _zelda: breath of the wild_. about 100 hours in, i’m happy to announce that princess zelda is home safe, and my love for this art form has been rekindled and reimagined. hell, i didn’t even consider it an art form before.

as soon as the ball dropped this year, i deleted social media for a while and became obsessed with the indie web, personal sites, and re-experiencing the internet that once was — before apps killed it.

i’m no web designer or developer, but i’ve got a lot of myspace under my belt, and this year alone i’ve redesigned this site three times. i don’t spend nearly as much time on the site as i do pedaling around the city or slaying lynels, but the few hours spent turning a vision into something real, in an unfamiliar medium, have been a real treat.

the biking, the gaming, the designing — they’ve all kept me sane. i’ve just been too proud to notice.

creativity, like god, is everywhere.
